Cyanoworld was an ambitious educational game. The project was a city-building simulation designed to teach players about renewable energy, sustainable agriculture, and eco-friendly living. Rather than just explaining concepts through text, the game aimed to let players *experience* what it takes to build and manage a sustainable city—choosing from solar panels and wind turbines to aquaponics systems and climate-adaptive housing.

The team created impressive visual assets, including charming low-poly 3D models of buildings, infrastructure, and characters, along with detailed concept art and game interface designs. They even developed a working interactive 3D demo with actual gameplay mechanics that was shown at public events. The project was comprehensive in scope, featuring complex systems for energy management, resource interdependencies, and climate-adaptive design—all with an educational purpose at its heart.

Despite the creative ambition and technical accomplishment, Cyanoworld ultimately wasn't completed. The combination of scope creep, the challenging economics of educational games, technical complexity, and limited development resources proved too much to sustain. Without a clear way to fund ongoing development, the passion project eventually concluded. This repository now serves as a historical archive, preserving the thoughtful design work and creative vision that went into imagining a game about building a better, more sustainable world.
