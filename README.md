## Project Overview

Cyano World was an educational game and city-building simulation focused on sustainable living and renewable energy. The project aimed to teach players about environmental sustainability, renewable energy systems, and resource management through an interactive, colorful low-poly 3D game environment.

## Project Concept

- **Renewable Energy Systems**: Solar thermal plants, photovoltaic arrays, wind turbines (horizontal and vertical axis), geothermal plants, wave energy systems, and hydroelectric dams
- **Food Production**: Traditional farming, aquaponics systems, chicken farms, and egg production
- **Water Management**: Rainwater tanks, desalination plants, and water distribution systems
- **Infrastructure**: Residential eco-houses with climate-adaptive designs, commercial stores, and population management
- **Educational Focus**: Teaching renewable energy concepts, sustainable agriculture, and climate-appropriate building design

## Development Status

### What Was Completed

- 3D models for all building types and infrastructure with technical specifications and dimensions
- Concept art and promotional materials
- Game interface mockups showing building selection menus
- Working interactive, realtime 3D tech demo with gameplay mechanics 
- Public demonstration at events (ie. playable prototype/ proof-of-concept)

### Game Mechanics Proposed

- **Energy Management**: Different power generation methods suited to various terrain types (hills, valleys, coasts, geothermal zones)
- **Climate-Adaptive Housing**: Three eco-house designs for different temperature climates
- **Resource Interdependencies**: Player progression from basic farming to advanced aquaponics, from solar to multiple renewable sources
- **Scalable Infrastructure**: Small and medium-sized versions of most facilities for flexible city planning

### Visual Assets

- Cute and appealing low-poly 3D models
- Consistent artistic style across all assets
- Multiple scale variations for buildings
- Character assets (eg. "blue guy" protagnoist anthropomorphised animals for farms)

## Timeline

## Why Development Stopped

1. **Scope Creep**: The ambitious feature set covering multiple renewable energy types, climate systems, and building mechanics required substantial ongoing development

2. **Educational Game Market Challenges**: 
   - Limited monetization opportunities in educational games
   - Difficulty reaching target audience (schools, educational institutions)
   - Competing with established educational game titles

3. **Technical Complexity**: 
   - Complex resource management systems with interdependencies
   - Dynamic environmental simulation (wind patterns, solar angles, geothermal zones)
   - Potential performance optimization challenges with low-poly assets

4. **Resource Constraints**: 
   - Development team likely limited in size or availability
   - Sustaining development of a passion project without revenue model proved difficult

5. **Platform/Technology Decisions**: 
   - Asset and design work is extensive, but actual game implementation progress is unclear
   - May have encountered technical limitations or tool/engine compatibility issues

## Archive Status

This repository preserves the project as a historical record. The extensive concept art, 3D models, and design documentation represent significant creative and educational work in game design focused on environmental sustainability.

## Legacy

Cyanoworld demonstrates the potential for using interactive gaming as an educational tool for teaching renewable energy and sustainable living practices. The striking design and concepts provides value for educators, game designers, and developers interested in educational gaming and environmental simulation.
